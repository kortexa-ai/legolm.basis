\begin{abstract}
This note expands on paper 7 (\emph{Zero Weight-Space Interference Is Not
Enough}~\cite{penov2026interference}), which closed additive composition of
sensor bricks on a frozen language model and left a constructive existence
proof with an open problem: oracle selection recovers every brick exactly
(8/8 seeds), but the only learned mechanism for engaging it --- a
task-conditioned router --- must be \emph{given} the task identity and
escapes its collapse basin in only 6 of 8 seeds. We replace the learned
router with a frozen text classifier: at evaluation, a lane selector reads
the eval item's own text surface and emits a hard one-hot over bricks,
composed exactly as the oracle's. Nothing trains through the LM loss, so
there is no collapse basin. Under a preregistered two-round protocol, both
selector arms --- a 175,107-trainable-parameter frozen 350M
encoder with a tuned head, and a 39,235-parameter byte-CNN ---
clear the registered roll-up at \textbf{8/8
seeds with retention 1.000 in both modalities}, against the cited
6/8 for the learned router. Both of paper 7's load-bearing
caveats fall at this scope. Three discounts travel with the claim: the
dispatch requires in-domain supervision, pretraining buys nothing at the
wired level, and the leakage exclusion is item-level because a text-level
rule would empty both task lanes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why this note exists}

Paper 7's constructive section ended one step short of a method. Selection
composes exactly: under \texttt{gate-oracle}, each brick's out-of-objective
probe returns to its single-brick score at every one of eight seeds. But the
learned task-conditioned router that engages selection carries two caveats at
paragraph prominence: it is handed the task identity through a learned
embedding (the sensor-feature stream carries no routing signal by
construction --- the feature-only null's between-modality $L_1$ gap is
exactly $0.000$ at all eight seeds), and its escape from the collapse basin
is seed-determined (6 of 8, always starving audio). The
registered follow-up was to make the escape \emph{selectable}. A substrate
bake-off (recorded in the program log) found that prompt-lane classification
is saturable by small frozen encoders where generation steering fails.
This note wires that selector in and runs the experiment the open problem
asked for.

\section{The experiment}

\textbf{One new gate mode.} \texttt{gate-selector} evaluates the same
composition as \texttt{gate-oracle} with one substitution: the one-hot over
bricks comes from a frozen lane classifier reading the eval item's own text
surface, not from ground truth. No task embedding exists, no gate steps run,
and nothing trains through the LM loss --- so seed variation can come only
from the bricks and the eval draw, which the oracle column already shows do
not decide anything. Two arms share the wiring: the registered substrate
(LFM2.5-Encoder-350M, frozen, LoRA rank~4 on attention plus a linear head;
175,107 trainable parameters) and a from-scratch byte-CNN
(39,235 parameters) as the pretraining control. Each selector is
trained once (seed 314159) and frozen across all eight harness
seeds (42, 1042, 2042, 3042, 4042, 5042, 6042, 7042), at the paper-7 Arm-5
configuration verbatim: three bricks (IMU, audio, vision) on disjoint
768-dimensional coefficient blocks over a shared frozen basis on
LFM\,2.5\,230M, 64 eval items per modality per seed.

\textbf{The integrity check.} On any eval item where the selector's argmax
equals the true brick, the composed weights must equal
\texttt{gate-oracle}'s for that item bit-for-bit, and the probe rank must
match; a violation raises rather than reports. Across both rounds,
2,344 correctly-routed items were checked and 2,344 matched
--- a rate of 100.0\%, by construction. Every gap between a selector column
and the oracle column is therefore selector error and nothing else: retention
is pinned to the ceiling wherever the classifier is right, and the
experiment's only free quantity is classifier accuracy.

\textbf{Leakage rule.} The eight seeds' eval items are enumerable up front:
1,024 items over
56 distinct text surfaces. Round 1 excluded
every training item whose text matched any eval surface ---
12,299 of 25,081 pool items
(audio 2,000, imu 10,299) --- and the selectors never saw an eval item.

\textbf{Two rounds, registered in advance.} The preregistration named three
readings and permitted exactly one iteration, gated mechanically: a launcher
script renders the round-1 verdict from the artifacts and refuses to run
round 2 unless it reads \emph{selector-limited} --- retention failing
exactly where the selector was wrong.

\textbf{Round 1 obtained the selector-limited reading.} The bake-off's
selectors, trained on a foreign rendering of the same lanes, do not transfer
to the harness's text surface: the encoder scores 57.8\% on
audio and 0.0\% on IMU eval items, the byte-CNN 0.0\% on both,
and both arms roll up 0/8. Retention tracks accuracy mechanically
(Table~\ref{tab:coupling}): encoder audio retention spans
52.4\%--77.3\% at 57.8\% accuracy, and IMU
retention sits at 17.9\%--25.0\% --- which is the
\texttt{others\_only} floor, because a wrong one-hot composes exactly the
\texttt{others\_only} control. Selector accuracy has zero seed variance by
construction: the eval text multiset is seed-invariant, so a text-only gate
is seed-\emph{constant} --- the opposite failure geometry from the learned
router's bistability.

\textbf{Round 2 saturates.} Retrained on the harness's own train-split
streams (item-level exclusion; for IMU no eval \emph{subject} is ever
seen), both arms score 64/64 on the eval texts of both modalities at every
seed, and retention is 1.000 in both modalities at all eight seeds ---
matching the oracle column cell for cell. The single-brick references the
retention is measured against are unchanged throughout (seed 42: IMU
0.750, audio 0.297, reproducing paper 7's Arm-5
comparator to the digit).

Both of paper 7's caveats fall at this scope. The gate is not given task
identity --- it reads the question --- and there is no bistability, because
there is no basin: nothing is trained through the LM loss, and the
escape-rate column for a text gate is a constant of the text, not a draw of
the seed.

\section{What does not follow}

\textbf{Dispatch needs in-domain supervision.} Round 1 is a real negative:
classifiers at 0.997--1.000 held-out accuracy on their own training surface
transferred at 57.8\%/0.0\% (encoder) and 0/0
(byte-CNN) to the harness's surface. The direction is symmetric --- the
round-2 encoder drops to 95.3\% on the template-stripped surface
it no longer trains on, the round-2 byte-CNN to 67.2\%. Nothing
here demonstrates zero-shot lane inference; the claim is that
\emph{supervised} dispatch, trained on the deployment surface, engages
selection exactly.

\textbf{Pretraining buys nothing at the wired level.} The
39,235-parameter byte-CNN matches the 350M encoder in every cell of
Table~\ref{tab:money}. At this scope the dispatch is a surface-features
problem, and the encoder's advantages live off-surface (transfer,
calibration), not in the wired result. A method claim built on the encoder
specifically would overstate what this experiment shows.

\textbf{The exclusion is item-level, not text-level.} The registered
text-level rule cannot apply to round 2: excluding every training item whose
text matches an eval surface would remove
7,352 IMU and
1,600 audio items --- both
task lanes in their entirety, recorded in the artifact as
\texttt{text\_rule\_would\_empty\_lanes}. Round 2's selectors have
therefore seen the eval \emph{label words} in other items --- never an
eval item, and for IMU never an eval subject. This is load-bearing for how
strong ``never seen'' is, and it is why the round-1/round-2 distinction is
reported rather than collapsed.

\textbf{Scope.} One model (LFM\,2.5\,230M), one brick geometry
(disjoint-768 over a shared frozen basis), two powered modalities, $n=64$
eval items per cell ($\pm 0.091$ binomial half-width at 95\%); each selector
was trained once at seed 314159 (MPS training is not
bit-reproducible, so the selector weights, unlike the splits, are not
reproducible artifacts); and the roll-up threshold is inherited from paper 7,
not chosen here.

\section{Consequence for the program}

Paper 7's open problem is closed at this scope, and its constructive section
upgrades from existence proof to method contingent on scale: selection
composes, and a text-gated selector engages it without task identity and
without bistability. The composition rail ladders before it lifts --- the
next rungs are selection over real hypernetwork bricks at 0.8B and above,
and more task bricks, where the collapse mechanism predicts learned routing
degrades and a supervised selector should not care.
